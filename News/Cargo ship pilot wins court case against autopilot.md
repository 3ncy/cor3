# Cargo ship pilot wins court case against autopilot

|-|-|-|-|

![](news/halcyon42.jpg)

The pilot of the cargo ship Halcyon-42 has won a legal dispute against the "Paradigm Logistics" shipping company after being suspended from flights for manually disabling the autopilot during approach to a cargo dock.

The incident occurred on approach to Khepri Transfer Hub. According to the carrier's official statement, pilot Martin Crow violated the automatic approach regulations and took control without dispatcher confirmation. The company classified his actions as "unjustified interference with a safe autopilot mode."

Crow himself claimed that the autopilot was guiding the vessel along an incorrect trajectory because of inaccurate mass data for the attached container block. According to the pilot, the system continued to treat the cargo as stable and securely fixed, even though one of the external containers had already begun to shift.

The court was given access to flight telemetry, dock radar data, and external camera recordings. The expert review confirmed that the autopilot had indeed been using an incorrect mass model and could have brought the vessel dangerously close to the dock's structural frame.

The court's decision states that the pilot had the right to disable the autopilot, since "human intervention was based on an observable risk that had not been correctly processed by the navigation system."

The company is required to restore Crow's flight clearance, pay compensation for the period of suspension, cover legal costs, and remove the violation mark from his personal file.

The manufacturer of the autopilot system, "VolgaSoft" stated that the court decision "does not call the safety of their system into question," but confirmed that it will update the cargo mass calculation algorithm.

Among cargo ship pilots, the ruling is already being called an important precedent. According to a representative of the transport operators' union, "this is the first case in a long time where a court has directly recognized that if a person sees automation making a mistake, they are not required to wait until the autopilot causes a disaster."
