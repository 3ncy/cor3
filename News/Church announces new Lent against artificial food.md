# Church announces new Lent against artificial food

| Source | | Date | Author |
|-|-|-|-|
| ![](news/dockside_signal_source_icon.png) | Dockside Signal | 2251-07-29 | Elena Martynova |

![](news/new_lent.jpg)

The Orthodox community of Novaya Ladoga Station has announced a new Great Lent, which has since been supported by the Greater Orthodox community, during which believers are encouraged to give up artificial food, synthetic proteins, and products made through a full laboratory cycle.

The decision was published by the parish council of St. Nicholas Church. The statement says this is not a ban, but a "voluntary spiritual restriction" meant to remind people of the connection between the body, labor, and living earth.

During the Lent, believers are encouraged to avoid synthetic nutrient masses, bioreactor cultures, fully processed nutrient blends, and products with no direct natural origin. At the same time, the parish specifically clarified that it does not condemn people who cannot afford natural food or who live in sectors where it is physically unavailable.

"Upholding tradition must not become a privilege of the wealthy," the statement says. "If a person eats artificial food not by choice, but out of necessity, there is no guilt in that. But if a person has the opportunity to give up, even temporarily, food created without soil, labor, and living nature, then they should think about what exactly they are feeding their body."

The decision has already sparked debate among residents of Novaya Ladoga. Supporters of the initiative describe the Lent as an attempt to give people at least temporary control over their own diet. Critics see it as a meaningless gesture against a reality in which most of the population of orbital stations depends on synthetic food every day.

Artificial food producers are responding cautiously. Nutrimentum stated that it "respects the religious practices of consumers," but emphasized that modern synthetic products go through a full safety control cycle. Unigroup declined to comment.

Economists are already warning that demand for natural products may rise during the Lent, especially in orbital cities and major transport hubs. On Novaya Ladoga, prices for fresh milk, eggs, vegetables, and organic meat began rising even before the official start of the Lent.

Parish representatives, meanwhile, urged people not to turn the Lent into a "status fair" and reminded them that its meaning is not in buying expensive food, but in voluntarily giving up a familiar dependency.

The Lent will begin next Monday and last forty days.
