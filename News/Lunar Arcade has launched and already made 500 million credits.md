Lunar Arcade has done what every independent developer dreams of: it released an unremarkable-looking indie game, and just a few days later ended up with a launch that many AAA studios would envy.

According to early platform estimates, Lunar Arcade has already generated more than half a billion credits in total revenue.

The gameplay itself is fairly simple: short, fast sessions, neon visuals, physics on the edge of chaos, and the constant urge to try one more run. No massive simulation, no claim to be "the new era of interactive worlds." Just a well-built arcade game that landed at exactly the right moment for an audience tired of soulless production-line releases.

The developers of Lunar Arcade thanked players and asked for "a little patience" while the team fixes the few remaining bugs and prepares an expansion.

At the moment, Lunar Arcade looks like that rare case where a small game stayed small in concept, but became huge in impact. And somewhere out there, major publishers are once again asking their analysts how they failed to notice it earlier.
