# Naked Party on Vesta Ring Station: guests lost their jobs, sponsors lost their dignity

A closed party on Vesta Ring Station has turned into exactly the kind of scandal after which sponsors suddenly remember the words "personal responsibility," "private initiative," and that they simply "walked through the wrong door."

The event took place on a rented luxury deck under the name Soft Eclipse. It was supposed to be a closed gathering for actors, streamers, young executives, heirs of wealthy families, media investors, tech geniuses, and people whose main claim to fame is that, for some reason, they keep getting invited to every expensive party.

Despite a strict no-recording rule, videos from the party leaked online.

By morning, the "closed gathering" had become the biggest scandal of the week. In the leaked clips, guests can be seen drinking from body folds as if they were glasses, dancing with the minimum possible amount of clothing, competing in dignity, breaking a decorative garden, and using the station's observation window as the backdrop for an improvised "modern art" performance. Some participants have already locked their profiles, several have deleted all of their posts, and at least a few employees of public companies have reportedly been temporarily suspended from work.

The guest list is still being debated, partly because many people in the footage were wearing masks, filters, body art, or were simply in a state that did not help with identification. According to media outlets and especially persistent internet detectives, the attendees included actors, heirs, influencers, nightlife investors, software startup founders, financial bloggers, and several people connected to private mercenary companies.

That last point quickly became its own separate scandal. Nobody is officially claiming that active contractors were present at the party, but the phrase "people connected to private mercenary companies" is being used very actively today.

Sponsors launched their crisis campaigns almost immediately. Drop Shot Energy Drink deleted two advertising posts featuring participants who were allegedly seen at the party. Space Monkey cigarettes stated that their products were "not officially provided for the event," which does not sound very convincing given the photo we have of the brand mascot statue in an absolutely obscene pose. Zingeli Exotic Foods, whose catering tags are visible in several leaked videos, said it "does not comment on the behavior of private clients after service has been completed."

The organizers of Soft Eclipse have not yet released a full statement. One person claiming to be close to the event team said the party "was never supposed to become public" and that people are "just pretending to be shocked after seeing rich people behave indecently."

Maybe that is true.

But it probably will not help anyone get their contract back.
